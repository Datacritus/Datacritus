Why DATACRITUS Exists

A reflection on evidence, assumptions, and why better questions might matter more than easy answers.

DATAINFORMATIONKNOWLEDGEWISDOM

So, what is this all about?

Let's make something clear from the very beginning.

It's a tool. Nothing more, nothing less.

An open, accessible tool designed to help anyone better understand the country they live in — in our case, and for now, Greece.

As a starting point, we've brought together around 500 indicators from nine official international data sources. From everyday social issues to long-term economic trends, from education and healthcare to employment, prosperity and democracy.

But the idea isn't simply to display numbers. It's to explore the stories behind them.

Every day, we're surrounded by information: news, opinions, statements, arguments, predictions and supposedly unquestionable facts.

We hear them from politicians, journalists, colleagues, friends, our parents and sometimes even our children. We inherit beliefs, assumptions and interpretations of the past, often without realizing how deeply they influence the way we see the present.

But how much of what we believe is actually supported by evidence? Is there a factual basis behind the arguments we repeat? Are things really getting worse? Are we perhaps doing better than ever before? And can either of those claims be justified?

The purpose of DATACRITUS isn't to declare opinions right or wrong. It isn't a machine for producing political verdicts or deciding who deserves credit and who deserves blame.

Its purpose is to encourage us to question. Not just to question others, but, perhaps most importantly, to question ourselves.

To challenge our assumptions. To look beyond a headline. To discover whether what we believe matches what the evidence suggests. And, hopefully, to push public dialogue a little further.

Because we live in a time when information is everywhere, yet understanding often feels increasingly difficult to achieve. The distance between having access to information and actually possessing knowledge can be enormous.

Data. Decision. Democracy.

Our motto is simple: Data. Decision. Democracy. But there is a deeper thought behind those three words.

Data, on its own, is not knowledge. It needs context to become information, interpretation to become understanding, and critical thinking to become knowledge — and perhaps, eventually, wisdom.

And maybe that's where better decisions begin. Not with the certainty that we are right, but with the willingness to question what we think we know.

Because democracy isn't simply about having the freedom to make choices. It's also about having the opportunity, the information and the judgment to make those choices thoughtfully.

Data can inform decisions. Better-informed decisions can strengthen democracy.

That's the aspiration behind DATACRITUS: not to tell people what to think, but to give them another way to think for themselves.

Where did the idea come from?

In the period leading up to 2024, Greece was approaching the 50th anniversary of the restoration of democracy — the Metapolitefsi.

Around the same period, I was completing my MSc, and the two things somehow came together.

I started thinking about how we discuss the last fifty years of our country. How we judge governments, compare generations, remember crises and celebrate achievements.

And how often those discussions rely more on inherited narratives than on measurable evidence.

That was, in a sense, where DATACRITUS was born.

Not as an attempt to rewrite history or settle political arguments, but as a way to make it easier for anyone to explore them with a little more context.

And democracy seemed like a particularly fitting place to start.

What can you do with it?

Explore it. Experiment with it. Challenge it.

Compare different periods, governments and indicators. Look for patterns, relationships, improvements and setbacks.

Some comparisons will make perfect sense. Others won't. Some indicators may move together, while others tell entirely different stories. And that's perfectly fine.

Correlation isn't causation. A number without context can be misleading. And a comparison isn't automatically a conclusion.

There are thousands of possible combinations, and undoubtedly some of the most interesting ones are those we haven't thought of yet.

Share your findings. Question our methodology. Report something you dislike or believe could be improved. Suggest new indicators, new comparisons or entirely new ideas.

This is a work in progress, and constructive criticism is more than welcome.

And finally, back to basics.

DATACRITUS isn't here to prove a point.

It isn't here to judge the past, defend the present or predict the future with certainty.

It's here to help us understand a little more. To inform. To educate. To encourage curiosity.

Perhaps even to help us put aside some of the anger, the political baggage and the unquestioned assumptions that we carry from one generation to the next.

Because democracy needs more than opinions. It needs informed citizens, open minds and the willingness to reconsider what we think we know.

Data can become information. Information can become knowledge. And knowledge, with enough reflection and humility, can eventually become wisdom.

If DATACRITUS manages to change even a few perspectives, inspire a few meaningful conversations or simply encourage someone to ask one more question before reaching a conclusion, then it will have achieved something worthwhile.

Not because the numbers have all the answers.

But because they might help us ask better questions.
